Contributing significantly to data breaches across organizations worldwide, e-mail phishing attacks remain as one of the most prevalent vectors of cyberattacks. Although methods involving ensemble learning have contributed in significant improvements for detecting phishing attacks by achieving high values of precision, recall and F1 score, with the evolution of using modern methodologies in phishing, the solutions of this threat go beyond accuracy. The redundancy in continuous adaptability and explainability in existing systems demonstrate an important barrier involving user trust and operational deployment. To be effective, phishing detection needs to be more than just accurate, it must also be understandable and adaptable. This survey explores how to build these smarter systems by combining ensemble learning with explainable AI (XAI). We organize the latest methods such as using advanced feature engineering, SHAP/LIME into a clear framework and tackle the biggest challenges-shifting threats, outdated models, and the lack of standard datasets for testing. Drawing on recent findings, we provide practical guidance for creating detection tools that security analysts can actually trust and use. The goal is to align future research with the real-world needs of cybersecurity teams and regulators.
