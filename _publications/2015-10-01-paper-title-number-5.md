The rise in digital identities has increased the risk and complexity of identity fraud, which calls for sophisticated detection and prevention mechanisms. Federated learning, a distributed machine learning approach, has been proposed as an effective solution to collaborative model training without explicit data sharing, hence solving privacy and regulatory issues. This extensive survey exhaustively investigates the confluence of federated learning and identity fraud detection, and synthetic identity creation which is a convergence that is still largely untouched in prior work. An extensive taxonomy of federated learning protocols and architectures specific to fraud detection applications are offered, examining their efficacy against adaptive attack channels and adversarial attacks. Additionally, the twin functionality of federated learning in both fraud identity detection and support for generating synthetic identities to enhance system testing and adversarial robustness is explored. By way of a critical examination of state-of-the-art methods, existing challenges are observed such as heterogeneity of data, and privacy leakage threats, and suggest new research directions to improve scalability, interpretability, and security. This extensive survey specifically offers a wholistic survey integrating insights from different domains and presenting a unified view on how federated learning can be used for next-generation identity fraud prevention and synthetic data creation. The conclusions assist researchers and practitioners in creating robust, privacy-conscious, and scalable solutions for the ever-changing landscape of digital identity management.
